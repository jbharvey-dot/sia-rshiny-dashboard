# sia-rshiny-dashboard
Interactive R Shiny dashboard analyzing research on bicycle access and student outcomes. Compares studies across attendance, travel time, academic performance, and enrollment using structured data, interactive tables, and visual indicators.
# Bicycle Access & Student Outcomes Dashboard
## Overview
I created this dashboard as part of my work in the SIA Certificate program at the University of San Francisco. The project looked at a simple question: can providing bicycles to students who live far from school improve their access to education?
To explore this, I reviewed previous studies on bicycle programs and organized their findings into an interactive R Shiny dashboard. The dashboard compares results across attendance, travel time, academic performance, and enrollment or dropout rates.
## The Dashboard
I wanted the dashboard to make academic research easier to understand and compare. Instead of looking through several studies individually, a user can see their major findings in one place.
I organized information on each study, including the country, sample size, research design, intervention, and results. I also used simple visual indicators to show whether studies found positive, mixed, or negative effects.
I built the dashboard using **R, R Shiny, Excel, dplyr, readxl, DT, and Plotly**. It also supported my larger research proposal, where I used a Difference-in-Differences approach to examine how a bicycle program could be evaluated in Kenya.
## Why It Matters
One of my biggest takeaways from the SIA program was that research is most useful when people can understand and apply it. Educational researchers often work with information from different studies, locations, and populations, which can make it difficult to see the bigger picture.
This dashboard makes that process easier by allowing researchers to quickly compare findings, see where evidence is strongest, and identify questions that still need to be studied.
For me, the project was an opportunity to combine economics, data analysis, and program evaluation with a real development issue. It also taught me how data can be used not just to measure an intervention, but to help make better decisions about where resources can have the greatest impact.
